Science & Awareness

Understanding Teen Sleep Disorders

Teen biology naturally shifts sleep phases late, but school schedules start early. Here is why sleep health matters.

What Happens When Sleep is Disturbed?

Chronic sleep deprivation in adolescents impairs brain development, lowers academic focus, spikes anxiety, weakens immunity, and increases emotional reactivity.

The Teenage Circadian Rhythm Shift

During puberty, melatonin secretion is naturally delayed by up to 2 hours, making it biological for teens to fall asleep later (e.g., 11 PM vs 9 PM).

Screen Blue Light Impact

Blue light from smartphones trick the brain into thinking it is daytime, suppressing melatonin release and disrupting deep delta sleep.

Teen Sleep Myths vs. Facts

Fact: "Catch-up sleep" disrupts your circadian rhythm even further, causing "social jetlag" on Monday mornings. Consistency is key.
Fact: Teen fatigue is primarily biological caused by shifted sleep cycles and early school bell timings, not lack of motivation.
Fact: Memory consolidation happens during REM and deep slow-wave sleep. All-nighters reduce memory retention by up to 40%.
